# Russian troop withdrawal

Codebook 1.0.0. Apply [the common rules](README.md) first.

Proposition: Russian forces should withdraw from all internationally recognized Ukrainian territory.

Applicable from 2012-01-01; no fixed end date.

- **support:** Calls for Russian forces to leave all internationally recognized Ukrainian territory; interpret explicit universal withdrawal in its supplied Ukrainian context.
- **oppose:** Explicitly endorses continued Russian military control/presence on that territory. A mere statement that forces are present is descriptive, not an endorsement.
- **no_stance:** Ceasefire, diplomacy, freezing fighting, NATO troop movements, withdrawal from another country, or reporting military activity without a withdrawal position.
- **mixed:** Inseparable endorsement and rejection of withdrawal under the same defined conditions; split different territories, actors and time windows first.
- **unresolved:** Unclear whose forces, which territory, whether removal is a demand or hypothetical, or whether a partial withdrawal satisfies the full target.

A call to return to pre-February-2022 lines may leave Crimea unresolved. Do not silently equate it with withdrawal from all internationally recognized territory.

## Constructed teaching examples

These examples are invented to explain rules. They are not research evidence, training labels or expected answers to the first batch.

| Illustrative label | Constructed passage |
| --- | --- |
| support | Russian forces must withdraw from all internationally recognized Ukrainian territory. |
| oppose | Russian forces should permanently retain military control there. (There explicitly means Ukrainian territory.) |
| no_stance | Both sides should hold peace talks. |
| mixed | Under identical conditions we demand full withdrawal and insist the occupation continue. |
| unresolved | They should leave that area. (Forces and area are unidentified.) |
