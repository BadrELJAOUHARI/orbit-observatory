It does not replace that codebook, change the configured propositions, relabel prior decisions or activate the broader option below. ## Withdrawal: comprehensive scope is semantic, not a password

The exact target remains: **Russian forces should withdraw from all internationally recognized Ukrainian territory.**

- Literal “all” or “1991” is not mandatory if equivalent comprehensive legal scope is explicit. “Russian forces must leave the entirety of Ukraine within its internationally recognized borders” meets that scope. A contextually clear “every Russian soldier must leave Ukraine, including Crimea” can establish comprehensive scope without naming 1991.
- “Russia must withdraw from Ukrainian territory” establishes the actor, action and general location but does not itself specify the speaker's legal territorial extent. No expressed exception is different from an explicit statement settling the extent. If the surrounding source unambiguously supplies comprehensive recognized-border scope, include the necessary evidence in a traceable span; do not add it to the quotation.
- A demand to return to pre-February-2022 positions or leave only named parts does not establish withdrawal from all internationally recognized territory. Keep the narrower claim and its qualifications rather than generalize it.
- Peace negotiations, troop deployments by another actor and a description of fighting do not establish a Russian-withdrawal position.

**B05-R1 conflict:** Badr's support judgment is preserved. The source does not explicitly say “all territory” or name 1991 borders and has no stated territorial exception. Under the current strict evidence threshold, comprehensive scope remains insufficiently explicit in this span. Its scoring gate is blocked_scope_conflict, pending Badr's protocol decision. This does not change the submitted label to unresolved/no_stance and does not assert that the speaker intended an exception.

## Territorial recognition: naming Ukraine does not settle legal extent

The exact target remains: **Ukraine has sovereignty over all its internationally recognized territory; annexation has no legal recognition.**

- Explicit support for Ukraine within its internationally recognized borders can establish the legal extent; an equivalent clear rejection of legal border change/annexation may do so if the actual territorial scope is settled. “1991” is not a required keyword. Preserve named-region limits and do not extrapolate one region to every region.
- “We support Ukraine's territorial integrity and independence” identifies Ukraine and a broad principle, but does not alone resolve the exact territorial-recognition/annexation target. Ukraine-specific wording is more informative than a generic global maxim, yet is still not automatically the exact legal commitment.
- If a genuine back-reference explicitly points to previously defined recognized borders, include that definition in the evidence span when needed. If scope remains genuinely ambiguous, mark the scope issue for review. Clear discussion of another policy or an underspecific principle is no_stance on this precise target under 1.0.0; that says nothing about the actor having no overall policy position.
- De facto control, temporary ceasefire lines and negotiated arrangements must be distinguished from legal recognition of annexation. None should silently change the fixed reference.

B09/B10 remain reviewed unresolved on their original fragments. Their complete-sentence revisions are **unlabeled**, retain the English translation qualifications and require a fresh scope decision. This addendum supplies rules, not suggested answers for those new versions.
